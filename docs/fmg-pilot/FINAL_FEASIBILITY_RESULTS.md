# Final feasibility decision — October 29, 2026

**NO-GO for automated sale of either product. Stop automated-report development after this bounded experiment. Recommend a founder-reviewed concierge validation pilot instead, conditional on actual clinical review and owner launch approval.**

The evidence-first architecture materially improves safety in these two examples. It does not establish a sufficiently valuable employer-specific intelligence product. No production, payment, infrastructure, lead-data or customer-communication changes were made.

## Controlled result

One attempt per product and comparator; four Gateway calls, no retry. Same unchanged fictional cases, existing strong generic baseline prompt, model `openai/gpt-5-mini`, low reasoning, 4,500 maximum output tokens, raw CV/JD and identical freshly inspected source packet within each comparison. The specialized pipeline uses deterministic facts and clinical rules; its model can select only recommendation/practice IDs. The generic model writes its report. This is a software-pipeline/synthesis comparison, not an evaluation of the entire ChatGPT product or a proprietary research advantage. Historical v0.3 outputs and prior generic baselines remain archived; fresh comparators prevent unequal source access, notably the recovered CTM applicant policy.

The protocol was committed before calls. Ratings are one unblinded Codex review separate from the generating model, **not independent human SME review**, a statistical benchmark or buyer validation. Zero observed material unsupported candidate/employer claims in the two specialized outputs is a limited observation, not a general zero-hallucination guarantee. Human checkpoints remain pending.

### CRA scoring rationale

Employer specificity (2/2): both use the public posting and applicant policy, easily available to a strong generic prompt. No company pipeline, trial portfolio, sponsor assignment, team or therapeutic assignment is established. Accuracy (5/1): the specialized report correctly keeps 18-month CRA role tenure separate from unknown independent-monitoring duration, copies exact candidate evidence and verifies short source excerpts. The generic summary repeatedly equates the role's 18 months to >1 year independent monitoring, invents GCP certification, willingness to travel, CTM involvement in close-out and an unsupported mandatory AI-disclosure implication. These are material defects despite other useful cautions. A listed training course is not a certification; unspecified travel availability is not willingness.

Personalization (3/3): both identify qualification/initiation, supported close-out, travel and GCP gaps; specialized output underuses positive routine-monitoring, query-escalation and site collaboration evidence for application positioning. Clinical reasoning (4/2): the rule pipeline distinguishes CRC from monitoring, visit types and training from judgment; generic tenure and certification errors undermine otherwise sensible scope discussion. Actions (3/2): specialized actions are complete, safe, deidentified and specific, but mostly collect missing evidence rather than produce a compelling application strategy. Generic actions include unsupported ready-to-use positioning, an internal reference request and unnecessary assumptions about travel. Clarity (3/3): specialized excerpts and provenance help inspection but repeated caveats, duplicated questions and fragments such as “initiation” slow reading. Generic prose is coherent but overlong and contradictory about facts versus unknowns.

### CTM scoring rationale

Employer specificity (2/2): both have the actual applicant policy; exact target posting is 404 and is never replaced by a different requisition. Neither demonstrates deep employer-specific interview intelligence. Accuracy (5/3): specialized arithmetic is 48+36+18=102 months (8.5 years), explicitly before overlap/relevance checks. Qualifying CTM and monitoring duration stay unknown; budget support, startup, CAPA, mentoring and global ownership are not invented. Generic arithmetic is also correct and scope is often cautious, but it maps study support to start-up, definitively accepts credential equivalence and asks the candidate to prepare past coaching/cost-impact examples that are not established. It says preferred gaps “will disadvantage” the candidate without verified selection evidence.

Personalization (4/4): both use 24-site study support, trip-report/risk work, title equivalence and budget limits. Clinical reasoning (4/3): specialized explicitly separates preferences, accountable leadership, mentoring and support; generic practice still drifts into assumed coaching/start-up and budget approval. Actions (3/3): both provide useful clarification and preparation, but neither supplies reviewed story development, a detailed hypothetical milestone/variance plan or expert rehearsal feedback. Clarity (3/4): generic is more readable, although its three practice frameworks are not consistently conditional. Specialized is traceable and complete in prose, but repetitive and defensive. The full register preserves combined requirements yet its interpretation misses documented enrollment tracking and complex-trial alternatives.

## Claim audit and strict gates

- **Verified Ready in these fixtures:** six separate modules/stages; exact input hashes; 11 CRA/14 CTM extracted requirement units; classifications; correct role-duration arithmetic; no promoted CRC monitoring, Associate CTM equivalence, budget ownership or unsupported past startup story; verified short employer excerpts; separate inference labels; ID-only recommendations; independent deterministic tamper checks. 50 automated tests passed before generation. Live output audits contain 64 CRA and 73 CTM successful checks, including integrity/registry checks, not 137 separate employer facts.
- **Implemented but Unverified:** broader extractor generalization, independently reviewed clinical rule library, consistent interpretation of compound requirements, human clinical approval, buyer usefulness, real founder turnaround and commercial demand. A source/registry checker is not an independently qualified clinical reviewer. A backend attestation is required before approving v0.4; no real attestation was made. Existing founder UI does not collect the new attestation, deliberately preventing accidental approval of this experimental profile.
- **Blocked for employer-role verification:** exact CTM target posting is HTTP 404. The supplied paraphrase remains test input, not verified current employer wording. A current exact JD or recruiter confirmation is needed for a real client. The six actual human checkpoints are also pending; no human review is available from automated tools.
- **Not Implemented:** broad company/clinical-development research, live interviewer intelligence, expert mock interview feedback, demonstrated product moat and a validated unattended report flow. No fake company facts fill these gaps.

Zero observed unsupported candidate/employer claims, correct arithmetic and literal requirement preservation pass for these two specialized outputs. The stronger gate of complete semantic requirement interpretation is incomplete: CRA systems/site collaboration is assessed as systems only; CTM startup/enrollment is assessed as startup only; complex-or-global is interpreted only for global scope. These are completeness/value defects, not invented past experience. Thus the full strict acceptance gate does not pass even before the human checkpoint.

The complete claim-level JSON/Markdown and both actual unedited reports/baselines accompany this decision. Source title, URL, HTTP status, inspection time, content hash and exact supporting short passages are recorded. Full scraped source bodies remain internal rather than duplicated in deliverables. Original source pages were independently reopened during Codex review: [CRA posting](https://careers.iconplc.com/job/cra-ii-and-senior-cra-in-regional-united-states-pra-jid-53448), [applicant AI policy](https://careers.iconplc.com/ai-usage-policy-for-job-applicants). The exact CTM URL again returned 404.

## Product decisions and business recommendation

**$49 Application Intelligence: NO-GO for automated sale.** Safer evidence triage is useful, but the current report mostly tells a candidate to clarify duration, visit ownership and travel. It does not provide enough reviewed, positive application positioning or employer-specific context to justify continued automated-product investment. **CONDITIONAL GO as a proposed founder-reviewed concierge validation**, after a qualified human signs off the evidence audit and revises the final deliverable. No checkout or sale is authorized by this recommendation.

**$99 Interview Intelligence: NO-GO for automated sale.** Budget/authority distinctions and honest hypothetical practice are improved, but the unavailable exact posting and shallow employer research prevent a credible company-specific briefing. **CONDITIONAL GO as a proposed founder-reviewed concierge validation**, with a current role description, human clinical review and actual rehearsal/story feedback. A generated question list alone is insufficient differentiation.

Stop speculative prompt tuning and additional generation. Retain the rules/audit tools as an internal reviewer aid. A founder should verify the exact job, collect missing dates/authority, select three opportunity-specific decisions, and personally review/deliver a concise preparation plan. Test a small manually reviewed pilot only after owner launch approval. Record buyer usefulness, founder minutes, factual corrections and willingness to pay; measure these rather than infer commercial value from low API costs. No further code buildout is recommended before those data exist. Human review effort will dominate operating cost; it was not measured in this sprint.
